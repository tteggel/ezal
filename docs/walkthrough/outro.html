Questions this page turned up

Explaining code line by line is a good way to find its loose ends. These are the ones met while writing this page. Each ends with the entry in the full log that holds the evidence. None is a demonstrated fault in the firmware, and nothing in the firmware was changed to write this page.

Questions the code raises

  • Were the calibration voltages measured? The calibration guide heads the four checked-in voltages “placeholder”, while the code calls them the installed values. They are also not what chapter 2’s resistor chain would deliver from the 2.0 to 4.5 V in the rotator’s manual. The table and the constant. B5
  • Can a held button miss its start? A lease never outlives its token, and a token outlives the next status report by exactly the 500 ms that a motor start needs. So for part of every half second a steadily held button has too little lease left, and a start that falls due then waits for the next hold: about a fifth of a second on a quick network, longer on a slow one. A comment beside the timing rules says they prevent this. Nothing unsafe follows. C6
  • Should the fastest rehearsed motor sit exactly on the stall check’s limit? The planner’s fast-motor rehearsals turn at 7° and 3° per second, which are also the speeds above which a reading is called implausible. With one count of flicker in the readings, eight of the sixteen recorded passes were refused for “implausible speed”, a reason that says nothing about pointing. T7
  • Is 18 laps a day a search limit or a sanity limit? The range checks accept orbits of up to 18 laps a day, and anything above about 17 would be underground. A damaged record at 17.9 passes every check and produces a tidy forecast with no passes, not an error. O2
  • How does anyone without a debug probe see a failed sensor test? Startup stops before the radio is switched on, so the only record is a log line, and the Pico’s light stays as dark as it does while Wi-Fi is still joining. B2
  • Is a fixed millisecond long enough for the sensor? Each reading waits 1 ms for a conversion that nominally takes 0.625 ms, without asking the chip whether it has finished. Slow the chip’s data rate without lengthening the wait and it would hand back the previous reading, looking fresh. B3
  • Which rule owns the instant a reading turns 500 ms old? At exactly 500 ms a manual move may continue and a tracking run stops. The stricter rule ends the run, so no harm is done. T9

What only measurement can settle

Chapter 7 lists the large ones: what a pin does while the chip restarts, how the dish really moves, how far it can turn, and whether the Pico is quick enough. The log adds three smaller ones.

  • Does the transistor pull the control box’s input low enough? The design estimates about a milliamp into its base. A lit LED shows what was asked for, not that the switch closed well enough to start a motor. C3
  • What does a quick tap do to the relays? The 500 ms minimum on-time does not apply to your own release, so a tap on the dashboard can ask the control box for a click a few milliseconds long, at most once every two seconds. C7
  • How long do a healthy reading and a normal start take? The page’s 20 ms read and “a few milliseconds” of loader are illustrations. Neither has been timed. B6, B8

Limits by design

  • Tracking is refused today. With the numbers checked in, the planner blocks every pass in the recorded orbit data: a 3.6° hop is too coarse for a 2° allowance, and no pass that crosses north has a route inside 0° to 360°. That is the planner being cautious with placeholders. T6, T8
  • Being in control is not being authenticated. Anyone who can reach the board on your network can take control when it is free, and the orbit data and the time arrive over plain connections that are checked for form, not for origin. C1, O6
  • The run-time tank counts only ezal’s own pins. Running the motors from the control box’s front panel is invisible to it. C2
  • The direction to a satellite is a straight line. It ignores hills, buildings and the bending of radio waves in air, and the seven-day limit on orbit data is a rule of thumb, not a bound on error. O4, O7

Where this page simplifies

  • Chapter 5 does not run SGP4. Its figures move the satellite round a plain ellipse with one steady correction. For the pass it follows that is within half a degree of ezal’s answer. Everything after that step was checked against the Rust and agrees to thirteen decimal places. O1
  • Chapter 2 draws an ideal sensor. A real one adds offset and noise, and a real feedback voltage is not perfectly straight. B7
  • Chapters 3 and 4 fix the network delay at 30 ms, and chapter 4’s live chart gives every hold a full lease. C8
